The idea

What changes when you zoom out?

Think of a photograph that reveals new patterns as you step back. In a quantum field theory, changing the distance you probe can change which interactions matter. At a fixed point, the effective rules stop changing with scale.

Supersymmetry is a symmetry that relates two kinds of fields, bosons and fermions. In the first two models in this paper, the long-distance rules can become supersymmetric after tuning to criticality. The third model is nonsupersymmetric at finite N. In the large-N limit, a set of its long-distance predictions matches those of the supersymmetric models.

Planar diagrams

Why the simplest diagrams win

A matrix entry carries two indices. Each free index can take N values, so a diagram with more free sums can carry more weight as N grows.

In this four-matrix example, pair neighboring entries, then try crossing the pairings. Watch the corner labels to see which indices have to be equal.

How this connects to the paper

After matching couplings, planar diagrams for observables shared by the three models agree in perturbation theory. The equivalence assumes the symmetries used to relate the models stay unbroken. Traces such as tr(φ2) and tr(φ3) are examples.

Equations behind the animation

Set a = λ1/[(4π)2ε], b = λ2/[(4π)2ε], u = λy2/[(4π)2ε] and τ = ε log(μ0/μ). At u = 16/5, the one-loop equations become

da/dτ = (3/5)a − 4a2 + 1/25 db/dτ = (3/5)b − 6a2 − 8ab − 2b2 + 3/25

In this slice, (1/5, −1/5) is stable and (1/5, −3/10) is a saddle. The stable point is the supersymmetric point of the SU(N) and SO(N) S2 models. The matched mixed-model fixed point shares the large-N flow after rescaling.

Scaling dimensions

A half-step apart

A scaling dimension tells you how a field’s correlations change with distance. At the matched planar fixed point, the fermion’s scaling dimension is exactly half a unit above the scalar’s in the displayed perturbative result.

Slide ε to move from four toward three spacetime dimensions. The curves use the paper’s expansion through ε3. The expansion is controlled near ε = 0. Its value at ε = 1 is an extrapolation.
