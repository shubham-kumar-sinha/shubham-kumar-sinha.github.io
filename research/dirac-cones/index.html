Band geometry

What makes a cone?

A vertical slice through the cone gives two straight bands crossing. Adding a Dirac mass separates the bands and leaves an interval with zero available states. The mass control updates both plots.

Graphene

One carbon sheet, two valleys

Graphene’s honeycomb has two sublattices. Their electronic amplitudes form a two-component wavefunction. Nearest-neighbor hopping makes its two bands meet at two inequivalent corners of the Brillouin zone.

Graphene’s two-component pseudospin tracks the A and B amplitudes. Electron spin is an additional degree of freedom. Castro Neto et al., The electronic properties of graphene

Topological-insulator surfaces

A conducting surface, a gapped bulk

A strong three-dimensional topological insulator has a bulk energy gap and can support a single Dirac cone on a given surface. On an ideal surface, the electron spin direction is tied to its direction of motion in momentum space.

Hasan and Kane, Colloquium: Topological insulators

The paper’s surface transition

When electrons start to pair

Place the chemical potential at the surface Dirac point and tune toward superconductivity. At the zero-temperature transition, the Dirac fermion and the fluctuating Cooper-pair field become partners under emergent supersymmetry.

Optical response

What light can tell us

Optical conductivity measures how the surface current responds to an oscillating electric field. At the critical point, scale invariance fixes the shape of the zero-temperature response. Supersymmetry fixes its relation to shear and entanglement.

Witczak-Krempa and Maciejko, Eqs. (3)–(5)

The missing thermal term

At finite temperature the response depends on the ratio x = ℏω/(kBT). When x is large, the stress tensor could contribute a term proportional to (i/x)³. The paper finds its coefficient is exactly zero at this supersymmetric critical point.
